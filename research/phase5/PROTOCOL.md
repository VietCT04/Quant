# PH5-001 initial development campaign

This first pass tests five-minute relative returns in six perpetual futures selected from the audited Liquid 50. It predeclares ten microstructure, regime, relative-value and lead-lag mechanisms, each evaluated at four trailing lookbacks (6, 12, 24 and 60 bars) and three gating levels (0.5, 1 and 1.5). The registered mechanism definitions and data-only payload are produced by `campaign.py`. The code uses completed five-second bars, forecast observations every five UTC minutes, an initial 122-bar warm-up, and observable coverage masks. A finite zero means no condition fired. Cross-sectional forecasts compare symbols contemporaneously. BTC leads by one observable five-second bar, and the subsequent five-minute return is the label.

## Selection and inference

- Screen 120 distinct configurations in both chronological folds, May 12–14 and June 9–11, 2026 (half-open UTC). Use five-minute close-to-close cross-sectional Pearson IC, with no labels that extend beyond either fold. Require IC at least 0.01 in both folds, at least 35 valid decision cross sections per fold and 95% finite-score coverage of active bars. Rank by the lower fold IC, breaking ties by family, lookback and threshold in ascending order. At most one configuration per mechanism and five total advance. All screening ICs are descriptive. Coverage includes inactive score zeros and does not imply an execution opportunity.
- Run untouched July 1–22 development validation for each selected configuration under both base costs and stress. Use cross-sectionally demeaned scores translated into independently normalized long and short USDT targets with total target gross 50,000 USDT, initial wallet 100,000 USDT, 1x leverage, 5% maximum bar participation and native funding. Submit zero targets when the row has fewer than four valid scores. Targets rebalance every five minutes. Baseline uses 5 bp fee, 2 bp quoted spread (half per fill), 1 bp slippage and zero extra impact coefficient. Stress uses 7.5 bp fee, 4 bp spread, 2 bp slippage and a 2 bp impact coefficient under the simulator's participation model. Simulator fills, funding, open exposures and marks determine results. Targets need not be fully filled. Compare with flat cash. Report gross PnL, fees, execution costs, funding and net PnL separately.
- July primary test is mean marked net daily PnL per 50,000 USDT gross target against a floor of 1 bp/day, using the last UTC mark per scheduled day and starting equity for the first difference. Require all 21 daily observations and usable marks, finite Bartlett HAC standard error with lag 2, and one-sided Student-t p-values with 20 degrees of freedom. Apply Holm at 0.05 across every base-cost July test selected before seeing July, assigning p=1 when inference is unavailable. Also require positive net stress PnL and that no single day provides more than half of the sum of positive base-cost daily PnL. Review warnings and execution behavior before allocating further research.
- August 1–15 development confirmation is available only to July survivors with unchanged strategy and parameters. Require marked net performance above 1 bp/day at base, positive net at stress, 14 marked days, and the same concentration limit. This is a directional robustness check without a second claim of statistical significance. An independent reviewer has yet to evaluate the protocol. Short samples, one venue and common crypto risk limit the evidence. Liquid 50 has survivorship and selection bias because it uses November 2026 activity.

Trade-flow, absorption and liquidation-like patterns use aggregate trade proxies. There is no observed order-book depth or liquidation identity in these tests. The mechanisms have independent falsifiable directions, but they can be correlated. The final evaluation window is excluded. No live trading or exchange connectivity is required.

Background work on order-flow mechanisms and lead-lag reasoning includes [Cont, Kukanov and Stoikov](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.6402) and [Lo and MacKinlay](https://www.nber.org/papers/w2977). They concern different markets or inputs. The hypotheses above are specific to the observable data in this campaign.
