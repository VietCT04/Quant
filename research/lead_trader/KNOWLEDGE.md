# Active-market inventory study | Binance portfolio 5140488422016171264

Portfolio **富一次就足够**. Record sources are the [public Binance portfolio page](https://www.binance.com/en/copy-trading/lead-details/5140488422016171264) and its public history endpoints. All timestamps below are UTC. This is a study of a published trader record and independent simulated hypotheses.

## Collection and provenance

- Anonymous read-only capture started November 27, 2026 UTC (November 28 local). Binance advertised about 14,000 position rows and returned 6,000 raw rows across 30 pages before throttling. Combining the earlier November 25 capture and deduplicating by **position ID and index**, keeping the latest timestamp, yielded **6,003 rows**, 424 repeating position IDs and 361 symbols. Updates run from October 3 to November 27. Some underlying positions opened earlier. Binance can reuse the same position ID across distinct lifecycles.
- Combining the earlier snapshot, date-filtered cursor pages and exploratory pages yielded **9,619 distinct executed-order records across 206 symbols**, from November 20 at 20:17 to November 27 at 20:03. Binance limited the general query to 6,000 advertised rows. The daily fetch recovered all 710 advertised records in a partial November 20 window, 2,200 of 3,616 on November 21 and 3,000 of 4,020 on November 22. Exploratory pages advanced page number as well as cursor and skipped blocks. Those records provide additional observations with known gaps. Daily files persisted after the first explicit throttle are empty or partial. Binance returned code `90801003`, “Too many requests. Please use WebSocket for updates.” The collector now aborts on explicit throttling. Counts by day describe the captured sample, and provide lower bounds on activity.
- Market matching uses the audited archive ending November 25, 2026. At least a minute of valid preceding market history was available for **1,702 of 5,810** in-range lifecycle opens across 47 symbols, **1,660 of 5,489** closes across 47 symbols and **1,621 of 7,869** order records across 26 symbols. Limit conclusions to this overlap. All 1,621 reported average prices fell inside the containing traded five-second bar's high/low within a 0.1 bp tolerance. This corroborates window alignment. It leaves intrabar ordering, earlier decision timestamps and execution queue position unresolved.
- Features use completed bars plus the SDK's 100 ms publication lag, within certified traded or confirmed-empty coverage. Quote activity compares per-minute turnover with the preceding nonoverlapping hour. Robustness checks include same-symbol previous-day same-clock pairs, BTC-adjusted returns, UTC-day splits and dropping the most sampled symbols. The prior-day reference can contain this trader's activity. These descriptive tests have clustered observations.

## Observed behavior

**Small market clips on both sides.** All 9,619 sampled rows were MARKET in one-way mode. There were 4,489 buys and 5,130 sells. Median filled value was **11.98 USDT** (10th and 90th percentiles 5.78 and 200.06 USDT). The captured count peaked at 3,200 order rows on November 22. In the 1,621 audited bar matches, median filled value was 0.26% of the bar's total traded quote value and the 90th percentile was 2.06%. Trade turnover alone cannot establish available depth. Records identify average executed prices and quantities at the order level. Order timestamps and update timestamps typically coincide, so the feed reveals little about actual order latency.

**Inventory changes and direct reversals.** There are 5,707 final closed rows and 296 partial rows. We reconstructed 132 closed lifecycles across 34 symbols with uniquely matched boundary orders within ±1 ms, positive intervening directional inventory, and reported peak and cumulative closed quantities. Each had at least one boundary order shared with an opposite lifecycle. A shared reversal's quantity is allocated using cumulative closed quantity and checked against the reported peak. This selects reversal-heavy and well-covered lifecycles. Of these, 36.4% had an add and 69.7% had a partial reduction. Median adds and reductions per lifecycle were 0 and 2. Median add size was 13.94 USDT across 389 adds, median reduction size was 28.72 USDT across 646, and median peak booked notional was 117.26 USDT. The median add was flat versus the last add and **12.5 bp favorable** versus weighted entry. Partial reductions were near weighted entry (median **+3.7 bp**, with almost half below it). These facts give weak support for unconditional averaging down or profit-only reductions. Wider public position rows suggest both sides recycled inventory. Closed volume exceeded maximum simultaneous quantity on 35.2% of long rows and 27.9% of short rows.

**Openings selected high activity, with asymmetric direction.** For 857 aligned longs, median signed returns in the prior 15 seconds, one minute, five minutes and hour were −5, −13, −18 and −52 bp. For 845 shorts the same measures were −4, −3, −2 and **+42 bp** (the hour often fell before the short). Minute turnover was 1.46× the prior hourly rate at long openings and 1.52× at short openings, compared with 0.71× and 0.73× at coincident prior-day controls. The paired activity difference was positive for about 72% of both groups. A negative median one-minute signed return appeared on 36 of 52 sampled days for longs and 30 of 50 for shorts (each day with ≥5 events). Dropping the three most represented symbols left median signed return −9.6 bp on 559 longs and −5.7 bp on 547 shorts. The symbol's five-minute return correlation with BTC at openings was 0.10 for longs and 0.15 for shorts. Median BTC-adjusted five-minute moves were −12.3 and −2.0 bp in the position direction. Median signed one-minute taker quote imbalance was −0.05 on longs and −0.01 on shorts. Five-minute realized log-price movement was about 2% around opens versus around 1% at controls.

**Opening and scaling patterns differ.** Five minutes after the pre-event close, signed median marked moves were −63 bp for long openings and −78 bp for shorts. These marks include the event bar, are sampled around repeated reversals and do not represent attainable markouts on individual orders. Conditioning on a prior adverse five-minute move of at least 30 bp still left a median next-five-minute move of −49 bp on 392 longs and −76 bp on 349 shorts. A prior move of at least 100 bp left −67 bp and −94 bp, respectively. On the 1,621 aligned order rows, median filled value fell across absolute one-minute-move bins: 47.16 USDT below 20 bp (511 rows), 35.66 at 20–50 bp (452), 35.42 at 50–100 bp (314) and 28.18 at ≥100 bp (344). This pooled association does not control for the trading symbol, portfolio state or order direction.

In the reconciled subset, 118 uniquely aligned adds saw a median signed one-minute move of −15 bp, while their preceding five minutes and hour were +43 bp and +1,785 bp. Of those adds, **100 were EVAAUSDT**. Long adds (33) had −76 bp over one minute and +452 bp over an hour, while short adds (85) had +4 and +1,950 bp. The 218 partial reductions occurred after moves favorable to the reducing order (+16 bp over one minute, +164 bp over five). In direction of the inventory these returns were negative. EVAAUSDT accounted for 136 reductions. These concentrated observations motivate testing but provide little confidence in cross-symbol generalization.

Of the 336 aligned add/reduce decisions, adds accounted for 47/133 after a one-minute move worse than −50 bp in the position direction, 18/59 from −50 to 0 bp, 13/58 from 0 to +50 bp, and 40/86 at +50 bp or greater. Removing EVAAUSDT changes those counts to 10/34, 2/20, 2/21 and 4/25. This supports testing controlled responses to adverse shocks, but the wider pattern changes considerably with symbol composition. The denominator consists only of observed inventory decisions. A probability of sending an order after an arbitrary market shock cannot be estimated from these counts.

**Profitable and losing positions.** Binance reported **+153,107 USDT** across 2,859 closed long rows, a 44.6% positive share. The five best longs contributed 129,108 USDT, equivalent to 84% of the long net sum. Across 2,848 shorts Binance reported **−5,663 USDT**, despite a 53.9% positive share. The five largest short losses totaled −31,077 USDT. Median hold was 290 minutes for longs and 208 minutes for shorts. Reported leverage had a median of 10× on both. Median peak value estimated at average cost was 174 USDT long and 148 USDT short. These are reported position outcomes and approximations, with unknown fee and funding decomposition. They cannot reconstruct account equity, margin use over time or daily portfolio Sharpe.

At audited exits, profitable longs had median signed prior five-minute and one-hour moves of **+21 and +97 bp** (396), versus **−25 and −172 bp** for losing longs (436). Profitable shorts had **+43 and +140 bp** (434), versus **−7 and −76 bp** for losing shorts (394). This is conditioned on a realized outcome and partly mechanical. The aggregate exit sample was more active than paired controls on about 72% of observations.

## Independent experiments

Two independent market rules test behaviors suggested by the data. Neither uses the lead trader's actions as a feature. Each uses completed one-minute data, a 60-minute trend ending five minutes before the decision, minimum observed turnover, 10 USDT orders, booked inventory, and controlled position growth. The pullback strategy adds only at new adverse prices while the lagged trend still supports the position. The impulse strategy requires recent movement in the trend direction and confirming taker imbalance. Partial reductions and exit rules are recorded in the frozen strategy. Long and short variants are separate tests. There is no leverage or execution-cost override.

The six-month Liquid 50 campaign **C-8d2815a4a0b3124f** registered H-0410–H-0414 on port 8765. Submission took 0.522 s. The frozen promotion criteria require positive net ROI and daily Sharpe, at least 200 closed lifecycles, and no material execution failures. Only qualifying variants advance unchanged to the two-year range. [First native screen](http://127.0.0.1:8765/api/hypotheses/H-0410/results)

## Confidence and open questions

- High confidence in observed market-order classification, strongly skewed size distribution, and exact five-second price consistency where covered. Moderate confidence in the descriptive activity relationship, given paired and day-level checks and symbol concentration.
- Low confidence in the source trader's private filters, sizing function, exact signal clock, margin allocation, use of order-book state and economic source of returns. The history feed was incomplete, and off-universe instruments make up most observations. The overlapping adds come overwhelmingly from one contract. Both account-level equity and all nontrading decisions are absent.
- If one side fails, preserve that as evidence against the specific rule. Small clips incur the full per-fill execution costs. Adding a burst of correlated orders does not establish alpha. The reported trader P&L and hypothetical backtests describe different portfolios, costs, leverage and data coverage.
