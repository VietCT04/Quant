# Active-market inventory study | Binance portfolio 5140488422016171264

## Signed bursts and state transitions · November 29, 2026

- In the [burst transition study](BURST_TRANSITIONS.md), the most reproducible directional market clue was a short adverse move before the first long ADD. In the marked minute panel its rate was 45/4,303 versus 103/28,475 overall, and the adverse move survived same-symbol matching. Short ADD and both first REDUCE hazards were elevated in both 15-second return tails. The market block did not consistently improve timing models on later dates and unseen symbols.
- Shorts more than 100 bp underwater chose an opposing BUY rather than a renewed SELL in 67/95 qualifying post-pause executions, versus 137/249 overall. Matched cost displacement and the chronological fit supported this association. The unseen-symbol fit supplied weak evidence, and the immediate post-ADD comparison did not support a simple loss-triggered switch.
- The frequency of same-side jumps from below $20 to at least $100 was higher after >30 seconds, especially for longs. Large jumps also occurred inside a run. Repeated fills at nearly identical prices weaken a strict nonzero grid spacing rule. Burst boundaries depend on chosen gap, and neither gaps nor order sides identify a private parent decision.
- A held-out comparison of opposing clip sizes against current inventory substantially improved reversal discrimination, but overpredicted actual reversal counts on later dates. Mechanical crossing and context-dependent sizing can coexist. Keep future research on fresh matched symbol-days focused on the long ADD signal, inventory-conditioned short switches and clip calibration.

## Focused action study · November 29, 2026

- The [confirmed inventory action study](ACTION_FOCUS.md) uses 6,035 eligible executions and 120,316 verified no-order minutes. The [ADD](ACTION_ADD.md), [REDUCE](ACTION_REDUCE.md) and [REVERSE](ACTION_REVERSE.md) reports keep action classification and timing as separate questions. Each links complete positions with counterexamples for manual review.
- **High confidence within the reconciled sample.** Recent action type and inventory state transfer substantially better across dates, symbols and lifecycles than unconditional class frequencies. Recent order size distinguishes small and large subsequent additions. The fitted price block gave no consistent gain. This leaves the private trigger and parent execution process unresolved.
- **Moderate confidence for the pooled exposure interaction.** In the adverse previous-fill price band, 72/123 executions reversed when exposure was at most $25, versus 1/300 when it was at least $250. Reductions accounted for 232/300 of the larger-exposure orders. The split repeats on later dates, but sparse same-symbol overlap and the executed-order risk set limit its use as a timing rule.
- **High confidence that both reversal paths exist.** Of 564 complete-path direct reversals, 213 followed at least three cuts and 177 flipped at least 75% of prior peak quantity without a preceding cut. Only three of the latter closed at least $250. An opposing clip large enough to consume remaining quantity is informative, but actual reversals seldom match the most recent trim size. Future work should compare new independently verified symbol-days and seek pre-trade book or parent-order evidence if it becomes public.

## Observed behavior

**Small market clips on both sides.** All 9,619 sampled rows were MARKET in one-way mode. There were 4,489 buys and 5,130 sells. Median filled value was **11.98 USDT** (10th and 90th percentiles 5.78 and 200.06 USDT). The captured count peaked at 3,200 order rows on November 22. In the 1,621 audited bar matches, median filled value was 0.26% of the bar's total traded quote value and the 90th percentile was 2.06%. Trade turnover alone cannot establish available depth. Records identify average executed prices and quantities at the order level. Order timestamps and update timestamps typically coincide, so the feed reveals little about actual order latency.

**Inventory changes and direct reversals.** There are 5,707 final closed rows and 296 partial rows. We reconstructed 132 closed lifecycles across 34 symbols with uniquely matched boundary orders within ±1 ms, positive intervening directional inventory, and reported peak and cumulative closed quantities. Each had at least one boundary order shared with an opposite lifecycle. A shared reversal's quantity is allocated using cumulative closed quantity and checked against the reported peak. This selects reversal-heavy and well-covered lifecycles. Of these, 36.4% had an add and 69.7% had a partial reduction. Median adds and reductions per lifecycle were 0 and 2. Median add size was 13.94 USDT across 389 adds, median reduction size was 28.72 USDT across 646, and median peak booked notional was 117.26 USDT. The median add was flat versus the last add and **12.5 bp favorable** versus weighted entry. Partial reductions were near weighted entry (median **+3.7 bp**, with almost half below it). These facts give weak support for unconditional averaging down or profit-only reductions. Wider public position rows suggest both sides recycled inventory. Closed volume exceeded maximum simultaneous quantity on 35.2% of long rows and 27.9% of short rows.

The timestamp evidence for reversals extends beyond the reconstructed subset. **5,341 of 5,707 closed rows (93.6%)** had a published opposite-side opening on the same symbol within ±1 ms. The counts were 2,698 of 2,859 long closes and 2,643 of 2,848 short closes. This strongly supports directional alternation in the published history. It does not identify the underlying triggers or establish that every adjacent record used a single order. The narrower order-reconciled subset is where individual reversal allocations can be checked.

## Independent experiments

Two independent market rules test behaviors suggested by the data. Neither uses the lead trader's actions as a feature. Each uses completed one-minute data, a 60-minute trend ending five minutes before the decision, minimum observed turnover, 10 USDT orders, booked inventory, and controlled position growth. The pullback strategy adds only at new adverse prices while the lagged trend still supports the position. The impulse strategy requires recent movement in the trend direction and confirming taker imbalance. Partial reductions and exit rules are recorded in the frozen strategy. Long and short variants are separate tests. There is no leverage or execution-cost override.

The six-month Liquid 50 campaign **C-8d2815a4a0b3124f** registered H-0410–H-0414 on port 8765. Submission took 0.522 s. ## Confidence and open questions

- High confidence in observed market-order classification, strongly skewed size distribution, and exact five-second price consistency where covered. Moderate confidence in the descriptive activity relationship, given paired and day-level checks and symbol concentration.
- Low confidence in the source trader's private filters, sizing function, exact signal clock, margin allocation, use of order-book state and economic source of returns. The history feed was incomplete, and off-universe instruments make up most observations. The overlapping adds come overwhelmingly from one contract. Both account-level equity and all nontrading decisions are absent.
- If one side fails, preserve that as evidence against the specific rule. Small clips incur the full per-fill execution costs. Adding a burst of correlated orders does not establish alpha. The reported trader P&L and hypothetical backtests describe different portfolios, costs, leverage and data coverage.
